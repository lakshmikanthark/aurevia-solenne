The thinking
behind Solenne.

An independent real estate website by Lakshmikantha. Strategy, design and frontend implementation, with AI assistance.

Explore the website

From browsing
to a shortlist.

The brief brings two tasks together: give visitors a feel for a place, then help them choose a home. Someone drawn in by the architecture should be able to compare usable space and sample costs, and carry that choice into an enquiry.

This is an independent concept for real estate businesses, built to demonstrate that complete journey.

Plan
the journey.

See the place

Large architectural scenes introduce the setting, interiors and evening atmosphere.

Compare homes

18 fictional residences can be filtered, sorted and compared. Interior space, outdoor space, total price and annual care stay distinct.

Keep the context

A chosen residence carries into the viewing preview. Visitors can add their priorities without starting again.

One visual
language.

Large images carry the story. Mineral paper, plum ink and smoked glass controls give the website a quiet, consistent identity. Short labels leave room for the architecture and the next useful action.

The experience begins with a full-screen wordmark and a moving architectural view. A curved page rises over that same photograph, leading into three centered image stories. One continuous horizontal composition carries oversized type, landscape and setting. Three photographic collection cards unfold into a fan of choices. An amenity scene carries into a curved reveal, then a pair of glass doors opens into the interior. Offset room images lead to two architectural crops that merge into a full-screen close. The gallery has its own horizontal filmstrip, while residence cards and inner-page images use shorter entrances that keep the selection and reading journeys clear. Day and Night change the atmosphere; smaller screens and reduced motion keep the content in a flowing layout.

My
contribution.

Lakshmikantha directed the brief, identity, information structure, visual design and frontend implementation, with AI assistance in development and writing. Native HTML, CSS and JavaScript, local assets and a static build keep the source inspectable and portable.

The architecture, setting and residence data are fictional. Architectural scenes are generated. This is not a client commission, and it reports no commercial result.

What success
would mean.

The intended business outcome is a more informed enquiry. A live project would need a baseline and agreed qualification criteria before any impact could be claimed.

Can people choose a suitable residence?

Observe collection selection and ask them to explain their choice.

Can they compare the figures correctly?

Check task accuracy for interior area, outdoor area, total price and annual care.

Can they complete the enquiry journey?

Observe residence context, useful questions and completion on mobile, with a keyboard and with reduced motion.

No user test results, conversion uplift, sales or revenue are claimed.

The
reference.

Era Residence, by The First The Last, informed the cinematic direction. Solenne has a separate identity, newly written copy, fictional data, generated imagery and independent implementation.

A supplied screen recording was first mapped into a separate, runnable sequence study. Its shared image-to-surface transitions, centered image stories, continuous horizontal composition and closing image expansion were checked in a browser before Maison Solenne’s content was merged into that structure. This makes the reference useful as a study of pacing, hierarchy and continuity. The final implementation is independent; exact source timing, affiliation and endorsement are not claimed.

For a
live project.

I would begin with approved property facts, media rights, buyer questions and the way your team handles enquiries. A live service would then connect the design to an enquiry receiver, an availability process and agreed measurement.
